The narrow recession

Between January 2008 and February 2010 the United States shed 8.7 million payroll jobs. The headline number reads like a flood that touched everything. It was closer to a sinkhole. Two industries took nearly half the losses, one industry never had a down month at all, and the places that fell hardest were the places that made things and built things.

A narrative visualization in four scenes. Built with D3, d3-annotation and TopoJSON.

Sources

Payroll employment by supersector, seasonally adjusted, monthly, January 2006 to December 2015: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Current Employment Statistics. County unemployment rates, at levels generally consistent with 2009: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Local Area Unemployment Statistics. Both distributed through the vega-datasets collection. County boundaries from the TopoJSON US Atlas, derived from U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundary files. BLS cannot vouch for analyses derived from its data after retrieval.

Built with

D3 v7, d3-annotation 2.5.1 and topojson-client 3.1.0, all served from this repository. No other visualization library is used. Recession shading follows the NBER peak of December 2007 and trough of June 2009.
